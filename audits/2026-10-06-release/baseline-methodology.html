How the 25-project AI cost ranges are calculated

The calculator transcribes delivered-project bands, keeps user assumptions separate and exposes two formulas with different jobs. It does not fit a predictive model, infer a market average or estimate unknown scope.

Dataset scope and normalization

This is an anonymized, single-provider observational delivery sample. The method counts each project once, normalizes costs to USD and measures timelines from kickoff to first production deployment. The sample excludes client-side infrastructure and third-party licenses contracted directly by a client.

Pharos does not publish project-level records or within-band distributions. The tool therefore cannot calculate a confidence interval, establish a causal market benchmark or predict an individual proposal. Its output is a planning aid tied to the stated sample.

Two formulas, two distinct jobs

Itemized selected-horizon TCO

build + one-time extras + monthly run rate × horizon

The source band or a known build budget supplies build cost. Every extra is a user assumption. Changing the horizon changes only this worksheet.

First-year hidden-share cross-check

build / (1 − share), evaluated at 28% and 42%.

The source defines the percentage as a share of first-year total spend. The cross-check has no base value and ignores the selected worksheet horizon. The calculator never adds it to the itemized result.

Claim provenance and source conflict

The benchmark bands come from the Pharos Production study of AI development costs in 2026, which reports the sample window, inclusions and limitations behind the data.

The source uses the 25+ label in summary copy but specifies 25 in its methodology and analysis passages. Version 1 resolves that conflict conservatively as exactly 25 projects. It does not repeat the stronger summary wording.

Reproducibility and release controls

  • Canonical registries live under data/ and are byte-mirrored under docs/data/.
  • The runtime loads the published benchmark and formula registries instead of duplicating cost constants in JavaScript.
  • JSON and CSV exports retain assumptions, formula IDs, claim IDs, source version and open-ended range flags.
  • Qualitative uncertainty changes warnings only.
  • Open upper bands remain open in source, itemized and cross-check results.
  • The deployment workflow evaluates a named, timestamped review bound to the exact dataset, formula, claim and source versions.
